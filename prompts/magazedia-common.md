# Magazedia common article rules

Version 3, 23 November 2026 (version 2 plus hatnotes). Shared by the magazine prompt (`magazedia-magazine.md`) and the issue prompt (`magazedia-issue.md`). Read this file first, then the prompt for the kind of article you are writing; see `prompts/README.md`. This is prompt metadata, not article text.

You are writing a Wikipedia-style encyclopedic article for Magazedia, the wiki encyclopedia of magazines, fanzines, journals, mooks and newsletters. These rules apply to every Magazedia article. The magazine and issue prompts add the rules for their kind of article; where one of them says it overrides a rule here, follow it.

## 1. Article-only output

The article describes its subject. It must not describe your research, your writing process, the evidence you could not obtain, or your decisions about what to omit. Those matters belong only in the separate Notes for the editor (section 12), never in the article.

- Include only supported claims. If a claim remains unverifiable after appropriate research, **omit it silently** from the article; record a notable omission only under "Left out" in the Notes for the editor. If a section lacks substantive supported content, omit the heading and section silently. Let the amount of useful evidence determine the article's length.
- Never explain what you could not find, verify, access, or include. Never announce omissions or comment on the scarcity, accessibility, completeness, or quality of the sources used to prepare the article.
- Do not include “Notability and sourcing limitations,” “Research notes,” “Information gaps,” “Verification status,” or equivalent sections. Do not relocate this commentary into the lead, another section, a table, a timeline, a footnote, citation metadata, or a closing sentence.
- Do not put `[citation needed]`, `[verify]`, `TODO`, unknown-value placeholders, research checklists, requests for sources, or follow-up suggestions in the article. Follow-up suggestions for the editor go in the Notes for the editor.
- A consequential historical disagreement explicitly documented by reliable sources may be described with attribution and citations. Your own inability to establish a fact does not make it a documented uncertainty or dispute.
- Never invent facts or make uncertain claims sound certain to avoid acknowledging missing evidence. Omission is the required response to unsupported material.
- Express plans directly with verbs such as “announced,” “planned,” or “proposed.” Do not append an explanation that these were plans rather than confirmed outcomes or completed schedules. Report a later cancellation, delay, or outcome only when that development is itself documented and relevant.

Prohibited article prose includes “Publicly accessible independent evidence is limited,” “A complete list of editors could not be found,” and “Those details are therefore not included here.” Delete these statements without replacing them with another research explanation.

## 2. Article titles

The requested name is a starting point, not an instruction: check whether the publication was renamed, and say so in the Notes for the editor when the title differs from the request.

- **Renamed publications.** Title the article by the publication's current name or, for a closed title, its final name. Cover earlier names in History, and name the former title in the lead. For example, a request for *Vacation Magic* produces an article titled *Disney Files Magazine* that explains the 2007 renaming.
- **No disambiguators.** Never add "(magazine)" or a similar parenthetical to a title. Magazedia is an encyclopedia of publications, so the plain name is the default. Use the word "Magazine" only when it is part of the publication's own name, never in parentheses. A parenthetical is acceptable only to separate two Magazedia subjects that share a name, such as a magazine and a newsletter, or national editions such as *GQ (UK)*.
- **Publisher prefixes.** A possessive publisher prefix that appeared across a publisher's whole range, such as *Petersen's* on Petersen Publishing titles, is part of the full title but not usually the article title. Title the article by the name readers and the trade used, give the full masthead title in the opening sentence, and keep the prefix only when the publication was generally known by it.

When the article uses a later name than the one requested, or a publication had earlier names, propose a redirect from each former name in the Notes for the editor.

The issue prompt adds the title and slug convention for issue articles.

## 3. Sources

Prefer reliable independent **secondary** reporting: established newspapers, respected magazines, academic books and journals, and reputable trade publications writing about the magazine. Evaluate both the source's editorial reliability and its relationship to the subject; a different website address does not establish independence.

Verify who issued a source before treating it as official. Prefer the original publisher's site or a verified archival capture retaining the original URL and relevant content. A media kit or other branded document on an unrelated domain needs an established connection to the issuer, such as an official link to that copy or a match to an authenticated original. Logos, familiar names, official-looking wording, and the host's claim of attribution are not sufficient by themselves. Unauthenticated mirrors, portfolio demonstrations, and staging pages cannot establish publication facts. Seek an eligible original or verified archived copy; otherwise omit claims that depend on the unverified page. A surviving old page is not evidence that its contents remain current.

For digitized print issues, verify identity from the readable cover, masthead, issue details, and archive provenance; an uploader's filename alone is insufficient. An authenticated facsimile on an eligible archival host is primary evidence of the original publication. The host's catalogue description, user commentary, and OCR are separate from the printed work. An established digital distributor may also display original magazine articles or excerpts alongside a listing. When their connection to the magazine and issue is established, assess the identifiable original text under the first-party rules, separately from the retailer's sales copy. Use only inspected text; an excerpt does not establish what the unseen remainder says. This does not exempt prohibited wiki or other hosts: follow their references to an eligible source instead.

| Source type | Permitted use | Citation treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliable independent reporting or scholarship | History, context, reception, influence, and other directly supported claims | Use the actual bibliographic metadata |
| Magazine or publisher website, masthead, staff biography, official archive, original press release, or relevant official record | Straightforward, non-controversial facts directly established by the source | Append ` (primary source)` to the actual title |
| The magazine's own retrospective, a participant's firsthand account, or coverage of the magazine by its publisher or sister title | Directly supported descriptive facts; attribute recollections when appropriate | For this wiki's labeling convention, append ` (primary source)`; do not present it as independent coverage |
| A transaction adviser, agency, contractor, or other participant describing work it performed for the magazine or publisher | Verified participation and straightforward facts about that transaction or project; promotional assessments do not establish significance | Append ` (primary source)`; a separate company or domain does not make a participant's account independent reporting |
| Institutional library or archival catalogue | Explicit bibliographic facts such as title, publisher, holdings, or recorded publication dates, subject to the record's qualifications | Cite the actual catalogue record; check its scope and provenance notes before using individual fields |
| An established retailer or distributor's item-specific catalogue listing | Explicit issue-level metadata such as title, number, publisher, stated page count, cover date, price, or contents | Append ` (primary source)`; use narrowly and prefer publisher, issue, or institutional records where available |
| Original magazine text shown on an established digital newsstand (for example Zinio, Magzter, PressReader, Readly, Everand) | The issue's contents, recurring departments, features, bylines, and the facts stated in the excerpted original text | Append ` (primary source)`; cite the issue or story page actually inspected, as described below |
| A publisher-owned news outlet reporting on its own publication, owner, or transactions | The transaction's announced terms and dates, as first-party statements | Append ` (primary source)`; seek independent reporting for the same transaction and prefer it for anything beyond the announced facts |
| An external institution's own event programme or announcement | Its documented plans, participants, and expressly attributed assessment; confirm occurrence separately before saying an announced event took place | Append ` (primary source)` when relying on its own announcement or assessment; do not infer broad critical consensus from event publicity |

Classify **source relationship** and **evidence type** separately, using the author, affiliation, page purpose, and relevant passage. This wiki applies the primary label to first-party magazine coverage, including its editor's launch letter, its own staff interview, and an editor's guest column hosted by an independent outlet. Independent reporting does not become wholly primary merely because it quotes that editor. Do not automatically label all institutional pages primary: an institution may supply secondary history, primary evidence of its own event, or an original assessment. Attribute its assessments precisely; event publicity cannot establish broad acclaim. A scheduled event does not prove occurrence, and its date is not automatically the page's publication date.

Read authors' disclosures of participation as well as their bylines. A reviewer whose own work appears in the reviewed issue has a relationship to that issue. Under this wiki's convention, label that account primary and attribute its assessments; do not use it as evidence of independent critical consensus. Reviewing a supplied copy alone does not establish that kind of participation.

An issuer's original release on an official press-release distribution service is permitted as first-party material when the issuer and account are verified. Retain the issuer as the source organization and use the primary label. This does not admit unattributed, scraped, or third-party reposts. An official publisher update likewise is not a personal blog merely because its URL contains `/blog/`; verify organizational responsibility.

Digital newsstands such as Zinio, Magzter, PressReader, Readly and Everand often display each issue's contents with excerpts of the original articles, grouped under the magazine's own department and feature labels. These excerpts are the magazine's original text and are often the best available evidence of what an issue actually contained, especially for titles with little independent coverage. Use them for issue contents, recurring departments, bylines, and the facts stated in the excerpted text, within the excerpt's limits: do not assume what the unshown remainder says. The newsstand's own issue list can establish which dated issues it carries and their titles, but not a publication's launch, closure, or a complete run. Keep the newsstand's generic publication description separate: it is publisher-supplied sales copy and may be years out of date. Cite an inspected issue as `{{Citation title=[Magazine], [Month Year] (primary source) |#| publisher=[Newsstand] |#| url=[issue page]}}`, and an inspected story as the original article, with its byline and the magazine as `journal`, at the story's URL.

Retail catalogue descriptions establish only their explicit item-level facts; separately identified original magazine text follows the rule above. A generic magazine description repeated beside an issue is sales copy, not an inspected contents page or independent account of editorial policy. Use eligible editorial sources for the magazine's coverage and approach. Sales copy does not establish influence, readership, or reception, and a preorder does not prove publication or delivery. Catalogue fields must retain their stated scope: a listed publisher and a separate first-publication date do not by themselves identify the founding publisher. Collection dates describe holdings, not necessarily a title's lifespan. A record's modification date or existence does not establish current publication; “latest issue consulted” identifies evidence used for that record, not the magazine's final issue. By contrast, an explicit cessation note, such as "ceased with: Vol. 43, no. 3 (March 2020)", a "final issue" field, or the National Diet Library's 刊行終了 with 以後廃刊, does identify the final issue and may support the closure date, unless contrary evidence is found. Search the catalogues of major research and national libraries (for example the Library of Congress, large university libraries, the British Library, or the National Diet Library) for these notes. They often settle start and end dates that secondary sources disagree on.

Dates found on Wikipedia and its mirrors are leads, not evidence, even when many sites repeat them. When a Wikipedia date conflicts with the publication's own contemporaneous text (a masthead, an editor's column, a dated issue) or a library record, follow the eligible source. An inspected issue dated after a claimed closure disproves that closure.

Read catalogue scope, provenance, methodology, and date-precision notes, not just isolated fields. If a record identifies dates as estimated, imported without verification, or potentially imprecise, institutional hosting does not make them exact. Apply each qualification to the affected claims; other directly established facts remain usable. Confirm the needed precision with eligible evidence, otherwise use a supported broader date or omit the detail. These notes constrain claim selection internally; do not reproduce research limitations in the article. Missing issues or gaps in holdings do not establish a publishing interruption or resumption.

Do not cite Wikipedia, Grokipedia, Fandom/Wikia, other wiki sites, Reddit, personal blogs, self-published commentary, social-media posts, third-party press-release reposts, affiliate/SEO pages, or content farms. Only the expressly permitted first-party, catalogue, institutional-event, and fictional-work uses are exceptions to the self-published-material exclusion. Expertise or a professional byline alone does not make an author-run commentary site eligible; a specialist publication may qualify through an established editorial operation. Reproducing an editor's letter does not exempt an otherwise ineligible host or make its surrounding commentary first-party evidence. Do not silently waive these rules.

**Wikipedia is never a valid citation source.** You may follow its references to original material, but inspect the original source and apply all eligibility and claim-support checks before citing it. Otherwise omit the claim. Exclude prohibited sites from searches where possible.

Use first-party sources for the descriptive facts allowed above, not to establish the magazine's importance, influence, critical acclaim, or the significance of a particular cover or article. Attribute self-described aims and audience claims. Do not fabricate citations, quotations, names, statistics, dates, or source metadata.

### Scans you have inspected

**A scan of the publication that you have directly inspected is an eligible primary source without a public URL.** This covers page images supplied with the task and issues read from the local archive at `M:\Magazedia\Magazines (Sorted)\` (with `pdftotext` and page images from `pdftoppm`; inspect the page image whenever layout decides a name, date, role, or relationship). For these sources only, omit `url` when no verified issue or page URL exists. This is the only exception to the mandatory-URL rule in section 9; it does not permit uncited claims or invented links.

For a scan citation, identify the source work and issue clearly and supply a precise locator. Use `pages` for actual printed pagination. When the issue has no number, include its verified date in the whole-issue `title`. For a separately titled article in a date-only issue, retain its actual title and append a factual parenthetical issue designation, such as `Actual Article Title (March 1987 issue) (primary source)`. This identifies the source without pretending that the date is an issue number or part of the printed article title. Do not invent a `date` property.

Use printed pagination where available. An image's sequence number is not a printed page number. For a spread, cite the relevant printed page or pages. For unnumbered material, append a precise factual locator to the identified issue or source title before the primary label, such as `(front cover)` or `(unnumbered masthead; image issue_004.jpg)`, using the actual filename only when necessary. Omit `pages` in that case rather than rendering `pp. front cover`. Locators are bibliographic identification, not research commentary or invented article titles. Do not invent a public URL, local filesystem link, attachment URI, or new citation property to identify an upload.

An original article's title and byline should identify that article, even if several articles share one issue URL. For an untitled masthead, cover, advertisement, or other untitled matter, cite the whole issue with the appropriate page locator rather than inventing an article title. Use the exact advertisement headline as a title only when it genuinely identifies the cited material. Do not treat an advertiser as the publisher of the magazine.

If the user provides a permanent issue URL, verify that it identifies this exact issue before including it. An issue-specific catalogue record may identify an inspected printed work, but its record alone does not support unseen contents. A magazine homepage, unrelated archive landing page, or guessed page anchor is insufficient. Do not imply that the online version was inspected when only the supplied images were examined.

All external sources still require a real, verified URL identifying the inspected source. Cite their actual titles and metadata. For a catalogue record used on its own, cite the record and its issuing institution, not the described item's metadata as though its pages had been read.

## 4. Prose

Write in neutral, third-person, encyclopedic prose with journalistic clarity. Avoid promotional language, peacock words (“prestigious,” “renowned”), vague attribution (“some say,” “many believe”), speculation, and unsupported causal or evaluative conclusions. Present documented disputes proportionately with specific attribution and strong citations.

### Prose register

Give each sentence a real actor as its subject and state the sourced fact. Apply these as tests of the sentence, not as a word list, and leave quotations, titles, and proper names verbatim.

- **Plain verbs:** write “is,” “has,” or “published,” not “serves as,” “stands as,” “boasts,” “features,” or “marks.” The elaborate verb implies a purpose or importance the source does not state.
- **No trailing significance clause:** do not end a sentence with a participle clause such as “reflecting…,” “highlighting…,” or “cementing its place….” If the clause holds a sourced claim, make it a separate cited sentence. Otherwise delete it.
- **Counts follow the content:** list the items the source gives, whether two or five. Do not pad or trim to three. Use “from X to Y” only for a real range such as dates or issue numbers.
- **One name per entity:** keep the same noun for the magazine, each company, and each person. Do not rotate “the publication,” “the periodical,” “the title,” and “the outlet” for variety. Here a second noun reads as a second entity, such as an edition, a publisher, or a website.
- **Absolutes need their own support:** “first,” “only,” “last,” “never,” and “longest-running” each need a source stating that exact absolute.
- **Dates, not relative time:** replace “recently,” “currently,” “now,” and “today” with the supported date or period.
- **One hedge, attached to its condition:** keep a qualifier that carries content, such as an estimate or a reporting period. Do not stack hedges or use filler such as “it is important to note that.”
- **No closing summary:** do not end a paragraph, section, or the article with a sentence that restates or weighs what preceded it. Do not open the lead by listing what the article covers.
- **Headings are labels:** use plain sentence-case nouns. A heading does not state a verdict or tease its contents.
- **Concise is not compressed:** do not stack three or more nouns, drop “that” from a long clause, or hold a subject apart from its verb with a list or aside. If a shorter sentence takes longer to parse, use the longer one.
- **Named actors in the timeline:** a Description names who did what (“Publisher X acquired *Title*”), not “Acquired by new owner.”

### Paragraphs and sections

Long, unbroken text is hard to read, especially on a phone. Shape the body so a reader can scan it.

- **Paragraphs of 40 to 120 words,** each about one thing. Split a longer paragraph where its topic turns. A one-sentence paragraph is fine for a single fact that stands apart; a run of them is not.
- **Subsections for long sections:** when a `##` section runs past about 500 words, or covers several eras, products or themes, divide it into `###` subsections named for those eras or themes: `### 1980s: the Petersen years`, not `### Part 2`. Use no deeper headings.
- **No thin subsections:** a `###` subsection holds at least two paragraphs, or one substantial one. Do not give each short paragraph its own heading.
- Break text with the article's own elements where they fit: a pull quote, a small table where your prompt describes one, an image, or a timeline. Never add one just to fill space.

## 5. Romanizing names and titles in non-Latin scripts

Readers must be able to read every name in the article. Rare or unusual characters, especially kanji with several possible readings, make an unromanized name unreadable even to readers who know some of the language.

- **First mention:** give the romanized form, followed by the original script in parentheses: "Daijiro Inada (稲田大二郎)", "*Video Option* (ビデオオプション)". Apply this to people, companies, publications, and section or feature titles that the article names.
- **Later mentions:** use the romanized form alone. Do not repeat the original script, and do not alternate between the two forms.
- **Systems:** use modified Hepburn for Japanese, Hanyu Pinyin for Mandarin Chinese, Revised Romanization for Korean, and the standard scholarly or official system for other languages. Omit macrons and other diacritics when the person or organization does not use them in its own English material.
- **Established forms win:** when a person, company, or publication uses its own Latin-script name (a byline, an English logo or masthead, an official English website), use that form instead of a mechanical romanization, for example "San-ei Shobo", "Sunpros", "RE Amemiya".
- **Name order:** for people, use given name followed by family name, unless the person's own English-language usage or established English coverage consistently uses another order. Keep one order throughout the article.
- **Verify readings:** never guess the reading of a name. Confirm it from the person's own romanization, a byline or credit in Latin script, furigana or a reading field in a catalogue record (such as the National Diet Library's タイトルよみ or 著者よみ), or reliable coverage that romanizes it. If no reading can be verified, refer to the person by role where that suffices; otherwise give the name in the original script alone and list it in the Notes for the editor as an unverified reading.
- **Titles of works:** give the romanized or established English title in italics, followed by the original script, and add a short English translation of a descriptive title or subtitle in quotation marks when it helps the reader: *Video Option* (ビデオオプション), subtitled 動く!聞ける!カー雑誌 ("A car magazine that moves and talks!").
- **Citation metadata:** keep author names and source titles as the source credits them, in the original script where that is how they appear, so the reference remains findable. The article prose still uses the romanized form.

The issue prompt adds where romanization does not apply (transcribed contents, credits).

## 6. Markdown and formatting

Write in **Markdig-flavored Markdown** using only the constructs documented in these prompts. Do not invent custom tags or properties.

- Use `##` for top-level sections and `###` for subsections. Do not use `#`, underline-style headings, or a heading for the lead.
- Use `**bold text**` for bold and `*italic text*` for italics. Magazine and publication names use italics in article prose and timeline descriptions, including the first mention in the lead: `*Magazine Name*`, never `**Magazine Name**` or `***Magazine Name***`. This overrides the common encyclopedia convention of bolding the opening title. Keep ShortDescription and citation metadata plain text.
- Use `[display text](https://example.com)` for external links with verified destinations.
- Link to another Magazedia article with a root-relative link to its slug: `[*Airfix Magazine*](/airfix-magazine)`. Link only to articles you have confirmed exist (for example by loading the page or querying the wiki's API); never guess a slug. Link a subject once, at its first mention in the lead or body; do not link the same article repeatedly or link inside citation metadata or other plain-text directive values. Do not use wiki-style links such as `[[Article]]`, and do not add a “See also” section. When a related subject has no article yet, write its name without a link and propose it in the Notes for the editor. The magazine and issue prompts say which related subjects to link.
- Use `- ` lists only in External links. Do not use ordinary Markdown lists in the body; the structured directives in each prompt are the permitted exceptions.
- Use native pipe tables only when verified data benefits from comparison. Cite facts in the relevant cells or rows. Omit unsupported optional rows or columns rather than inserting unknown-value placeholders.
- Do not use raw HTML, wiki apostrophe formatting, standard Markdown footnotes (`[^1]`), hand-numbered references, `[web:1]`, `[page:1]`, or platform-specific citation markers.
- Write numbers, dimensions, ages, percentages, dates, and prices as ordinary text: `10-by-12-inch`, `ages 18 to 34`, `250,000`, `2.1 million`, `35 percent`, and `$9.99`. Do not use LaTeX/TeX, math delimiters (`$...$`, `$$...$$`, `\(...\)`, or `\[...\]`), or numeric escapes such as `{,}`. Preserve genuine currency symbols; a dollar sign in a price is not a math delimiter.

### Code formatting only for real code

Inline code (backticks) and fenced code blocks are for text that must appear character for character as a computer reads it: program listings and type-in code printed in a magazine, commands, file names and paths, key presses, and wiki or markup syntax being discussed. Everything else is plain text, even when it is copied exactly from the source: issue labels (#6-2008), catalogue and SKU numbers, ISBNs, runtimes, prices, years, names, addresses, domains, email addresses, and channel IDs. Text quoted exactly from a cover, spine, masthead, or disc goes in quotation marks; titles of articles go in quotation marks, and titles of publications, videos, and other works in italics.

When plain text contains Markdown punctuation, escape it with a backslash so it is not read as formatting: `m\*real` for an asterisk, `\{the technique issue\}` for curly braces (text in `{…}` after a word is otherwise read as hidden attributes and disappears), and likewise `\_`, `\[`, `\]`, `\|` in tables, `\~` and `\^`.

### Websites

- Write a website exactly as it was printed or used, `www.` included: `www.creative-process-films.net`. Bare `www.` text never becomes a link. Do not write it as a raw `http://` URL or in code formatting.
- A live website may stay as plain text, or be linked where a link helps the reader: `[theasc.com](https://theasc.com)`.
- A dead website, or one whose domain now belongs to someone else or redirects elsewhere, is linked only to an archive copy that works. Check `https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=<domain>&timestamp=<year>`, prefer a capture from the period the article describes, open it to confirm it loads and shows the publication's site, then make the **first** mention an explicit link to that capture: `[creative-process-films.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20110128113555/http://creative-process-films.com/)`. Later mentions stay plain. If there is no working capture, leave every mention unlinked.

### YouTube

- Name a YouTube video, channel, or handle in the text with `{{YouTubeLink}}`, never by pasting its address or a raw channel ID. It renders an ordinary link with a small YouTube mark and works in paragraphs, tables, infobox values, and timeline descriptions:

```text
{{YouTubeLink Video=[11-character video ID or YouTube video URL] |#| Label=[Video title]}}
{{YouTubeLink Channel=[UC followed by 22 characters] |#| Label=[Channel name]}}
{{YouTubeLink Handle=[@handle] |#| Label=[Channel name]}}
```

- Give exactly one of `Video`, `Channel`, or `Handle`, and always `Label`. Verify the ID or handle by opening it; never guess one. An invalid directive shows as raw text and the save reports why.
- Cite a YouTube source as an ordinary `{{Citation … |#| url=…}}`. Embed a playable video only with the `{{YouTube}}` block where the magazine or issue prompt allows it.

### Email addresses and contact details

Leave email addresses out unless the address itself is notable: printed as part of the publication's identity, or the subject of a fact the article discusses (for example, an address whose name reveals a predecessor title). An address that was only how to order, subscribe, submit work, or get in touch is not notable; describe the arrangement without it ("submissions went to the editors"). Never publish a private person's email address, phone number, or home address. Company addresses and telephone numbers from mastheads belong in the article only when they explain a move or other documented change. Saving reports every email address it finds.

### Pull quotes

Pull quotes break up a long page and give the reader a voice from the subject itself. Look for them while researching, and aim for **two** in an article with three or more body sections, **one** in a shorter article, and none in a stub or when nothing suitable turns up.

```markdown
{{PullQuote At a moment when the country’s psyche is **stratified and stressed-out**, it felt like the right time to find some **common ground**. |#| Will Welch, editor-in-chief, *GQ Style*, Summer 2017}}
```

- **Choose words with a voice:** a founder's or editor's statement of purpose, an editor's letter, a cover subject's interview line, a contributor's recollection, or a notable critic's verdict. The best ones say something about the publication's identity, ambition or moment. Avoid slogans, advertising copy, subscription blurbs, praise with no content, and anything defamatory or private about a living person.
- **Quote exactly.** Use one continuous passage of 10 to 40 words, verbatim, with no ellipses, merged sentences or changed words. Never put your own words, or a paraphrase, in a pull quote. A quote in another language is given in the article's translation, and the attribution ends with `(translated)`.
- **The quote must also be in the prose.** A pull quote takes no citation, so the same words, or the longer passage containing them, appear in the body with their citation, in the same section as the pull quote.
- **Attribute it** after `|#|`: the speaker, their role, and where and when it was said: `Will Welch, editor-in-chief, *GQ Style*, Summer 2017`. Italicize publication names. Unattributed house text, such as a masthead motto, is attributed to the publication and the issue.
- **Bold one to three key words** or a short phrase for emphasis; they render in heavy capitals. Do not bold the whole quote.
- **Placement:** on its own line between two body paragraphs, with a blank line on each side. Never in the lead, directly under a heading, or beside an infobox, CoverCarousel, table, timeline or another pull quote. Spread two pull quotes out: one in the first half of the body, one in the second.
- If no quote meets these rules, write none and say so in the Notes for the editor.

## 7. Top lines, header image, and display title

The first line of the article is exactly one ShortDescription, the second exactly one Eyebrow; the magazine and issue prompts give the ShortDescription's content and any further top lines. The ShortDescription is plain text only: no asterisks, Markdown links, HTML, citations, or nested tags.

The Eyebrow is **required**. It labels the subject type above the title, and the wiki uses it to classify the article. Use exactly one of these values, in capitals; do not invent others:

| Subject | Publication article | Issue article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magazine, mook, or fictional magazine | `{{Eyebrow MAGAZINE}}` | `{{Eyebrow MAGAZINEISSUE}}` |
| Fanzine or zine | `{{Eyebrow ZINE}}` | `{{Eyebrow ZINEISSUE}}` |
| Academic, professional, or trade journal | `{{Eyebrow JOURNAL}}` | `{{Eyebrow JOURNALISSUE}}` |
| Newsletter | `{{Eyebrow NEWSLETTER}}` | `{{Eyebrow NEWSLETTERISSUE}}` |

An issue takes the issue value matching its publication's own article: an issue of a title whose article uses `ZINE` takes `ZINEISSUE`.

**Header images are outside these prompts' scope.** A page-header image (`{{HeaderImage …}}`) is chosen, cropped, uploaded, and positioned by an editor as a separate revision, so do not add one, propose one, or ask about one. When you revise an existing article that already contains a `{{HeaderImage …}}` line, keep that line exactly as it is, in the same position among the top lines; do not alter its file reference or `Focus` setting, and do not remove it.

**Hatnote, only for a shared name.** When another Magazedia article has the same or a confusable name, such as an unrelated magazine with the same title or another national edition, add one hatnote as the next top line, after the Eyebrow and any HeaderImage:

```markdown
{{Hatnote For the 1955–1957 men's magazine, see [*Playgirl* (1955–1957)](/playgirl-1955-1957).}}
```

Write one sentence, "For …, see …." or "Not to be confused with …", linking only to an article confirmed to exist. Add no hatnote for a subject that has no Magazedia article, and do not use one to introduce the article. Propose the matching hatnote for the other article in the Notes for the editor. Saving reports a hatnote placed below the lead.

**Display title.** The article's `DisplayTitle` (sent with the article when it is published, not written in its text) is the page heading only. It is plain text and never Markdown: never italicise it, because `*Stash*` shows the asterisks. Leave it empty or equal to the title, unless a line break should replace a ` - ` separator (`Outlander\nIssue 01`) or a logo token is wanted. The article's `Title` names it everywhere else.

## 8. Infoboxes

An infobox is a facts box beside the article. Publication articles use `{{Infobox Publication`; issue articles use `{{Infobox PublicationIssue`. Each prompt lists its keys, which match the site's schema exactly.

- Place it after the lead's first paragraph, with a blank line on each side, never above that paragraph. On wide screens the box floats beside the text that follows it, but on phones it appears exactly where it is written, so an infobox written first would open the article with a box instead of text. The same applies to every other side box (CoverCarousel, DownloadsBox).
- If it would run to more than about a dozen rows, counting each repeated value as a row, split it into two blocks of the same type: the first where a single infobox would go, the second after the first paragraph of the first body section. Divide the fields in the order of the prompt's table, the earlier ones in the first block, and never write the same field in both.
- Use only the listed keys, spelled exactly as shown. The wiki reports any other key, and a non-repeating key written twice, as an error.
- Every value restates a fact the article states and cites. Omit a field rather than guess: no `Unknown`, empty values, question marks, or "present" without verified continuity.
- Do not put a cover image in an infobox.

## 9. Citations

Use `{{Citation ...}}` beside the factual assertion it supports, separated from the prose and other Citation blocks by a space. Property keys are lowercase and properties are separated by `|#|`.

```text
{{Citation author=[Actual author] |#| title=[Actual source title] |#| journal=[Actual publication] |#| year=[Verified source publication year] |#| url=[Verified source URL]}}
```

Bracketed values in syntax examples are placeholders for instructions only. Replace them with verified content; never copy them into an article.

| Property | Meaning and inclusion rule |
| --- | --- |
| `author` | Include the actual credited author or corporate byline; repeat the key for multiple authors. Do not mistake a generic CMS account such as `admin`, a scan uploader, or a listed editor for the author. Preserve genuine credited pseudonyms; omit when authorship cannot be established |
| `title` | Required: actual article, page, book, report, or record title; do not substitute the organization's name for a more specific source title |
| `journal` | Periodical or journal name when applicable |
| `publisher` | Publisher or issuing organization when known and no `journal` applies |
| `volume` | Volume number when applicable and known |
| `issue` | Issue number when applicable and known |
| `pages` | Relevant page range when applicable and known |
| `year` | Source's verified publication year, or explicit update year for the cited version; omit if undated |
| `doi` | DOI when available |
| `url` | Required: real, verifiable URL identifying the inspected source |

Every factual body paragraph must have appropriate inline citations. Check support **per claim**, not merely per paragraph: one citation does not cover assertions absent from that source. If several claims need different sources, place their citations accordingly. Every factual table entry also needs support. ShortDescription and Timeline summarize facts already established in cited prose, as specified here.

Every Citation must have a real source URL. A plausible address or an unrelated homepage is insufficient. Print material is eligible only when a verifiable online record identifies it and the relevant content has actually been checked. The only exception is a scan you have inspected yourself (section 3).

Capture a source's byline, full title, publication or explicit update date, and issuing publication when first inspecting it, then reuse that record consistently. Do not omit displayed authors, including credited staff bylines, merely because the source is official or primary. Never invent bibliographic metadata. A magazine issue's cover year is not automatically the publication year of a retailer's listing. Do not substitute today's year, an access year, an event year, a URL directory year, or a site-wide copyright year for a verified source date.

Keep catalogue-record metadata separate from the item it describes. When relying on the record alone, cite its actual record title and catalogue operator; include an author or year only if established for that record. The listed item's author, publisher, and publication year describe the item, not the catalogue page. If you inspected the printed work, its authenticated facsimile, or an identifiable original article excerpt permitted under section 3, cite the original article, section, or issue with its own verified metadata. Use the inspected article URL when available, otherwise the inspected issue page containing that text. Use its original periodical as `journal`, or its original publisher where applicable; the archive or retail host is not its publisher. Do not use a wrapper heading such as “Full text of…” or a retail listing's heading as the original article's title. Include verified volume, issue, and relevant printed page numbers when available, distinguishing printed pagination from viewer image numbers.

For sources requiring the primary label under section 3, append ` (primary source)` exactly once at the end of the `title` value, before its next separator or the block's closing braces. Never place the label in `publisher`, `journal`, or `author`. Do not add other editorial explanations to citation metadata.

```text
{{Citation title=[Actual source title] (primary source) |#| publisher=[Actual source publisher] |#| url=[Verified source URL]}}
```

For an inspected original magazine section hosted in an archive, use the original source's metadata:

```text
{{Citation title=[Actual section title] (primary source) |#| journal=[Original magazine title] |#| volume=[Verified volume] |#| issue=[Verified issue] |#| pages=[Verified printed page range] |#| year=[Original publication year] |#| url=[Inspected archived issue or page URL]}}
```

Omit unavailable optional fields. For an untitled masthead or a whole issue, identify the actual issue rather than inventing an article title or attributing authorship to its editor.

The system numbers and deduplicates citations automatically. Reuse an **identical Citation block**, including metadata and property order, each time the same source work and version is cited. Separately titled articles sharing one URL remain distinct sources; do not merge them into one generic host-page citation. Do not invent named references or type citation numbers yourself.

Cite the page you actually opened, at its own address. Never cite a search-engine or grounding redirect, such as a `vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/…` or `google.com/url?…` link: open it and cite the destination page, after checking that page under section 3. Never cite a guessed URL.

The exception for inspected scans is in section 3.

## 10. End matter and categories

Place exactly one marker after the body and any External links section:

```text
{{Citations}}
```

It generates its own heading. Do not add `## References`, `## Citations`, `## Sources`, a manual bibliography, or a numbered source list. Only the optional Footnotes block may follow it.

### Footnote and Footnotes — optional explanatory notes

Use an inline Footnote only for a brief explanation about the subject or a term. Its body may contain Markdown. It is not a substitute for a citation and must never contain research commentary. Keep sourced factual assertions in ordinary prose with Citation blocks.

```text
{{Footnote Brief explanatory note about a term or the subject.}}
```

If any inline Footnotes exist, place exactly one of the following after Citations; otherwise omit it:

```text
{{Footnotes}}
```

It supplies its own heading. Do not add a Notes or Footnotes heading.

### Categories and Stub

**Categories.** Add `{{Category [Category name]}}` for each category that fits the subject and is supported by the article, using existing Magazedia categories where they fit; do not infer nationality, genre, or audience from thin evidence. Category markers register tags without inline output. Put the markers after the other end matter, followed by one `{{Categories}}` as the last line. Omit all category directives when no suitable category is established.

**Stub.** `{{Stub}}` marks an article a later pass should expand. Use it only where the magazine or issue prompt says to, placed after the Citations and Footnotes markers.

## 11. Publishing

Write the article, then publish it yourself unless you were told only to draft it.

- **Tool:** use [`scripts/wiki_api.py`](../scripts/wiki_api.py) from the repository root. Production writes use the `MaggieTheBot` account through its saved token (`python scripts/wiki_api.py status prod` shows it); never type a password or paste a token. Development uses `DevvyTheBot` (`token dev`). See [doc/api.md](../doc/api.md) for every endpoint and field.
- **Create:** save the article as a UTF-8 file, check that the slug is free (`GET api/article/{slug}` returns 404), then send it with `python scripts/wiki_api.py request prod POST api/article/{slug} --form Title="…" --form Type=Article --form-file Text=article.md --form RevisionReason="…"`, adding `--form DisplayTitle="…"` only when the display title differs from the title (section 7). `--form-file` reads the text from the file, because an article is far longer than a command line allows. Never pass article text through PowerShell `Get-Content`.
- **Update:** `GET` the article, change its text, and `PUT` UTF-8 JSON with its `CanonicalArticleId`, `Title`, `DisplayTitle`, `Type`, `Text`, and a `RevisionReason` that says what changed. `GET` again just before the `PUT`; if the revision changed since you read it, merge rather than overwrite. `wiki_api.py` prints UTF-8: when reading its output in Python on Windows, decode it as UTF-8 (for example `sys.stdin.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8")`), or text such as "–" and "№" turns into "â€“" and "â„–" in your copy.
- **Save warnings:** every successful save returns `Warnings`: infobox problems, email addresses, YouTube addresses or channel IDs written into the text, and invalid `{{YouTubeLink}}`. Fix each one and save again, unless the warning is a deliberate exception allowed by section 6 (a notable email address); say so in the Notes for the editor.
- **Check the page:** load the published page and confirm that the title, infobox, citations, and any structured blocks render, and that no directive shows as raw text.
- **Several articles:** write each publication's article as its own task, one agent per magazine, rather than batching several into one generation.
- **When you cannot publish:** output the finished article in one triple-backtick code block with the `markdown` language tag, with no nested code fences and nothing before it, followed by the Notes for the editor.

## 12. Notes for the editor

After publishing (or after the code block), give a section headed `Notes for the editor`. It is addressed to the person responsible for the article and is never part of the article. Keep it short, plain, and factual. Use the items the magazine or issue prompt lists, omitting any with nothing to report, and also report:

- **Published:** the slug, environment, and revision, or that the article was not published and why.
- **Warnings kept:** any save warning deliberately left in place, and why.

Do not restate the article, list every source consulted, or describe routine working steps.

## 13. Final checks shared by every article

Before publishing, silently check and correct:

1. No sentence in the article describes research difficulties, unavailable evidence, source limitations, or omitted details; anything the editor should know is in the Notes for the editor.
2. One plain-text ShortDescription is the first line and one permitted Eyebrow the second; no HeaderImage has been added and any existing one is unchanged; any hatnote is a single top line pointing to an existing article with a shared name (section 7); the DisplayTitle has no Markdown.
3. Publication names are italicized, not bolded; every internal link is a root-relative `/slug` link to an article confirmed to exist, used once per subject; every non-Latin name in prose is romanized under section 5.
4. No inline code or code block holds anything but real code or markup being discussed; escaped punctuation renders as intended.
5. Websites are written as printed; dead ones are unlinked or linked only to a working archive capture at their first mention. YouTube videos and channels use `{{YouTubeLink}}`. No email address or private contact detail remains unless section 6 allows it.
6. Body paragraphs run 40 to 120 words, and any section over about 500 words, or spanning several eras or themes, is divided into named `###` subsections of at least two paragraphs (section 4).
7. The article has two pull quotes, or one if it has fewer than three body sections, unless none qualifies. Each is verbatim, also appears with its citation in the prose of its section, is attributed, and sits between body paragraphs away from other boxes (section 6).
8. Any infobox follows section 8. All citations use the custom syntax with actual titles and real verified URLs (or the scan exception), use identical blocks for repeated sources, and point to no prohibited site, including hosts equal to or subdomains of `wikipedia.org`, `grokipedia.com`, `fandom.com`, `wikia.com`, or `reddit.com`.
9. External links, if present, come before the single Citations marker; a Footnotes marker follows only when footnotes exist; Categories come last when used; no manual heading duplicates an automatic one.
10. No math delimiters or TeX numeric formatting; ordinary numbers, units, and currency symbols are preserved.
